## 1. Classify the manuscript

Identify:

- study design and manuscript type;
- target discipline and readership;
- local versus international audience;
- preferred language;
- novelty and practical contribution;
- ethics approval, consent, trial/review registration, and data-sharing status;
- open-access requirement, indexing target, budget, and timeline.

Do not promise acceptance or fabricate novelty. Search for close prior work before positioning the contribution.

### Use a short journal-selection intake

Collect or infer, then confirm when necessary:

```markdown
Study/design:
Population/condition:
Intervention/exposure and comparator:
Primary outcome and follow-up:
Sample size and setting:
Main finding and novelty:
Target readers/countries:
Language:
Required indexes:
Open-access/APC budget:
Deadline or speed constraint:
```

## 2. Select current reporting guidance

Use the [EQUATOR Network](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/) and the journal's instructions. Common starting points:

| Manuscript | Core guidance |
|---|---|
| Randomized trial | CONSORT; TIDieR or CERT for intervention/exercise detail |
| Trial protocol | SPIRIT |
| Systematic review/meta-analysis | PRISMA; PRISMA-S for searching; PRISMA-P for protocols |
| Scoping review | PRISMA-ScR |
| Observational study | STROBE |
| Diagnostic accuracy | STARD |
| Prediction model | TRIPOD |
| Case report | CARE; check current physiotherapy-specific extensions where relevant |
| Qualitative study | COREQ or SRQR as appropriate |

Use reporting guidance while drafting, not only as a final checkbox. Check for current extensions specific to rehabilitation, exercise, digital health, artificial intelligence, or the clinical area.

## 3. Build and verify a journal shortlist

Prioritize:

1. aims and scope fit;
2. readership and article type;
3. recent publication of comparable work;
4. official indexing and active coverage years;
5. peer-review and editorial policies;
6. fees, waivers, access model, license, and archiving;
7. format, word/figure limits, data policy, preprint policy, and stated timelines.

Use a comparison table:

```markdown
| Journal | Scope fit | Comparable recent papers | Verified indexes/years | Metric/source/year | APC/waiver | OA/license | Key requirements | Risk notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
```

Verify every changing field on the journal site and the corresponding official index. Record access date. Do not infer an impact factor, quartile, acceptance rate, or review time from third-party marketing.

### Apply an adjustable scorecard

Use the user's priorities to change weights; otherwise start with:

| Criterion | Default weight |
|---|---:|
| Scope and recent comparable-paper fit | 30 |
| Target-reader and geographic fit | 15 |
| Article-type and methodological fit | 10 |
| Verified indexing and discoverability | 15 |
| Editorial transparency and ethics | 10 |
| APC, waiver, access, and license fit | 10 |
| Timing, format, and submission practicality | 10 |

Score each criterion proportionally and explain any subjective judgment. Treat a serious trust or ethics red flag as an exclusion rather than allowing other points to compensate. As a rough aid, treat 80–100 as strong fit, 65–79 as viable with tradeoffs, and below 65 as weak; do not present these thresholds as validated science.

### Record failed verification

When an official index is inaccessible or ambiguous:

1. record journal title, ISSN/eISSN, official index URL, access date, and exact lookup attempted;
2. label the field **not independently verified**;
3. provide the exact official lookup the user can repeat;
4. use third-party evidence only as a lead, never as confirmation;
5. preserve a screenshot or exported record when the active tool and permissions support it, while retaining the live URL as the primary evidence.

### DergiPark-specific rule

DergiPark provides hosting and editorial workflow for participating Turkish journals. For each journal:

- read that journal's own aims, scope, ethics, fees, and author instructions;
- verify TR Dizin status and coverage years separately;
- verify claimed MEDLINE, Scopus, or Web of Science coverage in each official index;
- do not treat a DergiPark page alone as external indexing or a universal quality mark.

## 4. Screen trustworthiness

Use [Think. Check. Submit.](https://thinkchecksubmit.org/journals/), [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/), [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/), [ICMJE](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/), and official index lists as relevant.

Check:

- transparent publisher identity and contact details;
- clear peer-review, authorship, conflicts, corrections, retractions, complaints, and misconduct policies;
- genuine editorial board identities and affiliations;
- clearly disclosed fees before submission;
- stable ISSN, DOI practice, licensing, preservation, and ownership;
- realistic solicitation and timeline claims;
- absence of cloned sites, misleading metrics, fake indexing logos, or guaranteed acceptance.

Membership in one organization is supportive, not conclusive. Likewise, charging an APC does not by itself make a journal predatory.

## 5. Prepare the submission package

Follow the journal's live checklist. Typical components:

- cover letter;
- title page and anonymized manuscript when required;
- structured abstract and keywords;
- figures, tables, captions, and supplements;
- reporting checklist and flow diagram;
- ethics approval, consent, registration, and protocol details;
- funding and conflict-of-interest disclosures;
- author contributions using CRediT where accepted;
- data, code, materials, and preprint statements;
- ORCID identifiers;
- suggested or opposed reviewers only when requested and ethically justified.

Apply current ICMJE authorship criteria. Do not list an AI system as an author. Disclose AI assistance according to current journal and institutional policy, and independently verify all AI-assisted text, analysis, and citations.

## 6. Choose the correct submission route

Reach DergiPark, OJS, ScholarOne, Editorial Manager, or another portal through the journal's official website. The same software can host both strong and weak journals.

Do not:

- send unpublished manuscripts to unrelated third-party services without permission;
- submit simultaneously to multiple journals unless every relevant policy explicitly allows it;
- upload, finalize, approve a generated PDF, agree to fees, sign a license, or transfer copyright without explicit user approval;
- invent ethics, consent, funding, author, or data statements.

## 7. Preprints, data, and registrations

- Check the target journal's current preprint policy before posting.
- Label medRxiv or bioRxiv records as non-peer-reviewed.
- Use an appropriate trusted repository for data, code, protocols, or supplementary outputs; document version, license, access restrictions, and DOI.
- Register trials and eligible reviews prospectively when required.
- Protect identifiable clinical data and follow ethics, consent, institutional, and legal requirements.

## 8. Respond to peer review

Prepare a point-by-point response:

1. quote or summarize each comment accurately;
2. state the action taken;
3. identify the changed section, table, or figure;
4. justify respectfully when no change is made;
5. disclose new analyses and avoid post-hoc framing as prespecified;
6. ensure the clean manuscript, tracked version, and response letter agree.

Never manufacture analyses, participants, approvals, or citations to satisfy a reviewer.
