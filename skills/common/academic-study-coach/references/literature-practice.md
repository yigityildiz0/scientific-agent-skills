# Literature literacy: turn the guide into practice

Use when the learner asks how to search/read/synthesize literature, alongside medical-evidence-research for actual retrieval/appraisal. Teach at the learner’s current level; do not force a systematic-review workflow onto an introductory exercise.

1. Ask for the learning target and relevant clinical/topic question if missing. Choose PICO, PECO, PCC or a qualitative framework; do not force every component into the search string.
2. Give one worked example and one learner attempt: Turkish concept to English synonyms/controlled vocabulary, then platform-specific search. The learner explains why terms are joined and what filters would miss.
3. Distinguish database/index/repository/journal host/registry. PEDro is intervention-focused and its /10 score is not an effect size; DiTA is for diagnostic accuracy. DergiPark hosts publications; it is not itself a guarantee of quality.
4. Build a reading matrix: verified identifier/location; design/sample/country; dose/comparator; outcome/time; effect+CI; bias; relevance to Türkiye. Keep quoted finding, paraphrase and learner inference in separate fields.
5. Practice synthesis by comparison/theme rather than author-by-author summaries or counting positive studies. Use claim → evidence → interpretation → limitation; verify citation style against the actual source/journal. Do not invent a DOI or convert an abstract into a full-text reading claim.
6. Use active recall: explain the difference between record/report/study; funding and bias; CI and SD; statistical and clinical importance; evidence certainty and recommendation strength. Correct the reasoning, then retest with a new example. Label all illustrative numbers as fictional.
7. For academic slides, preserve methods, units, uncertainty and claim-adjacent citations. Use audience-readable charts; distinguish an editable presentation from HTML/PDF screenshots.

Derived teaching exercises from the user’s literature guide v2 (source sections 13–14, 74–80, 81–98, reviewed 2026-10-11). This reference contains a new teaching workflow, not a copy of the source PDFs. Actual literature findings require an executed, documented search.
