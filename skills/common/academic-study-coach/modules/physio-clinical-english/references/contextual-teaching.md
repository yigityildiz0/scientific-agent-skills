# Contextual terminology teaching

## Terminology source order

1. [World Physiotherapy glossary](https://world.physio/resources/glossary)
2. [WHO ICF](https://www.who.int/classifications/international-classification-of-functioning-disability-and-health)
3. [COSMIN terminology](https://www.cosmin.nl/)
4. Operational definition in the paper's methods
5. [NLM MeSH](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/mesh/meshhome.html) and, when appropriate, SNOMED CT

If sources differ, explain domain and context. Do not imply one universal translation.

## Deep term card

Use only fields that add value:

- **English term / acronym**
- **Pronunciation cue** when the user asks
- **Turkish or requested-language equivalent**
- **Plain-language meaning**
- **Meaning in this exact text**
- **Clinical or methodological importance**
- **Word family and common collocations**
- **Frequently confused concept**
- **Short rehabilitation example**

Do not turn every repeated word into a card. Explain once, then use a consistent equivalent.

## Statistical sentence anatomy

Parse in this order:

1. Who or what is compared?
2. Which outcome, instrument, direction, and time point?
3. Which effect measure?
4. What does the point estimate suggest?
5. What clinically important benefit, no difference, or harm remains compatible with the 95% interval?
6. What does MIC/MCID, MDC/SDC, baseline risk, or scale range add?
7. Can the design support a causal claim?

For `p > .05`, never translate “no statistically significant difference” as “the treatments are identical” without interval-based equivalence evidence.

## Academic verbs and certainty

| Phrase | Preserve as | Do not strengthen to |
|---|---|---|
| is associated with | is related/associated | causes |
| may improve | possible improvement | definitely improves |
| suggests | provides a signal | proves |
| no evidence of | not demonstrated in this evidence | evidence that none exists |
| clinically meaningful | important under a stated threshold/context | important for every patient |
| predicts | estimates future probability | diagnoses or causes |

## Measurement language

- `reliability`: consistency/agreement under repeated measurement;
- `validity`: whether interpretations for the intended construct/use are supported;
- `responsiveness`: ability to validly detect change over time;
- `diagnostic sensitivity`: proportion with the target condition who test positive;
- `measurement error`: variation not attributed to true change;
- `MIC/MCID`: context-dependent important difference/change estimate;
- `MDC/SDC`: change beyond a defined measurement-error threshold.

## Clinical-language boundaries

- Translate `physical therapist diagnosis` according to local professional context; do not silently convert it into a medical disease diagnosis.
- Preserve `screen`, `assess`, `classify`, `rule in/out`, and `update probability` distinctions.
- Keep `precaution`, `relative contraindication`, `absolute contraindication`, and `stop criterion` separate.
- Translate patient-reported experience as experience; do not pathologize it.

## Instruments and copyrighted text

Keep instrument names, versions, and scoring direction. Use an official validated translation when verified. Label an explanatory translation as unofficial. Do not reproduce copyrighted questionnaires, scoring forms, or large source passages without permission.

## Visual teaching choices

Use a small table for repeated mappings, a flow for a process, a timeline for evidence change, or a Mermaid diagram for relationships with three or more branches. The visual supplements—not replaces—the full explanation.
