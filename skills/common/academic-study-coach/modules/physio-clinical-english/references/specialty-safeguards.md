# Specialty and setting safeguards

Use this as a prompt to widen screening—not as a diagnostic checklist. Verify current, condition-specific guidance and local escalation pathways.

## Musculoskeletal, trauma, and postoperative care

- Respect surgeon, tissue-healing, weight-bearing, range, wound, graft, fixation, and device restrictions; if unknown, do not guess.
- Escalate plausible fracture, infection, acute neurovascular compromise, compartment syndrome, deep-vein thrombosis/pulmonary embolism, cauda equina/conus syndrome, or rapid neurological loss.
- Neck trauma or pain with new cranial-nerve, cerebellar, visual, speech, swallowing, severe headache, or long-tract features is not a routine manipulation scenario.
- Return to work/sport is a risk decision using healing, symptoms, capacity, task exposure, cognition, environment, rules, and shared decision-making—not one test or elapsed time alone.

## Neurology and vestibular rehabilitation

- New focal neurological deficit or suspected acute stroke/transient ischaemic attack requires emergency action now. New seizure emergency, rapidly progressive weakness, altered consciousness, or an acute severe headache with concerning features also follows the emergency pathway—not routine same-day physiotherapy.
- For spinal-cord injury, anticipate autonomic dysreflexia, orthostatic intolerance, skin injury, temperature dysregulation, bowel/bladder complications, and equipment/transfer risks. Possible autonomic dysreflexia is an emergency: stop stimulation/exercise and activate the person's established emergency plan/local emergency service.
- Check swallowing/aspiration, communication, neglect, cognition, fatigue, falls, caregiver capacity, and decision-making support when relevant.
- Acute vestibular symptoms with central neurological features are not safely classified by one bedside maneuver or remote self-test.

## Cardiorespiratory, acute care, and critical care

- New or worsening chest pain, severe/unexplained dyspnoea, syncope, haemoptysis, cyanosis, acute confusion, or marked vital-sign deterioration requires medical escalation.
- Interpret oxygen saturation, heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, symptoms, trend, device accuracy, prescribed oxygen, altitude, diagnosis, and clinician-set limits together; do not use one universal number as clearance.
- Confirm lines, drains, airway, oxygen, anticoagulation, haemodynamic stability, mobilization orders, staffing, and emergency response before acute-care mobility.
- Cardiopulmonary tests require a suitable setting, equipment, monitoring, termination criteria, and trained response—not improvised home testing for an unstable person.

## Oncology, haematology, palliative care, and bone health

- Cancer/spinal pain with new weakness, gait loss, sensory loss, or bladder/bowel dysfunction suggesting metastatic spinal cord compression is an oncological emergency requiring immediate emergency or designated oncology-pathway contact. Suspected pathological fracture, sepsis, uncontrolled bleeding, hypercalcaemia, or rapid neurological decline follows the current urgent/emergency pathway according to presentation.
- Consider lesion location/stability, bone load, blood counts, infection risk, thrombosis, cardiotoxicity, neuropathy, fatigue, cachexia, skin, ports/lines, and treatment cycles.
- Align activity with goals, symptom burden, prognosis, dignity, caregiver resources, and palliative priorities; “more exercise” is not automatically the right goal.

## Pregnancy, postpartum, and pelvic health

- Screen current obstetric/postpartum warning signs and local urgent pathways before exercise or manual/procedural advice. Do not normalize severe headache/visual change, chest pain, severe breathlessness, heavy bleeding, fever, unilateral leg swelling, seizure, or marked deterioration.
- Pregnancy and postpartum status changes thrombosis, haemodynamic, pelvic, abdominal, healing, loading, and safeguarding considerations; use current obstetric guidance.
- Internal pelvic examination or treatment requires appropriate professional training and scope, explicit ongoing consent, privacy, trauma-informed practice, infection-control, and an appropriate in-person setting. Never give do-it-yourself internal examination instructions.

## Children and adolescents

- Use developmental age, growth, communication, family context, assent plus guardian consent, safeguarding duties, school/play demands, and pediatric norms.
- New regression, unexplained bruising/injury, systemic illness, suspected abuse/neglect, cancer warning, or acute neurological/cardiorespiratory change requires the relevant pathway.
- For suspected abuse/neglect, preserve immediate safety, use factual non-leading communication, and follow the local safeguarding pathway. Do not confront a suspected caregiver when that could increase risk or ask the child to investigate/manage the situation alone.
- Do not transfer adult dose, equipment fit, outcome thresholds, or return-to-sport rules without pediatric evidence and qualified oversight.

## Older adults, frailty, cognition, and multimorbidity

- Consider delirium, falls, orthostatic symptoms, polypharmacy, sensory loss, bone health, skin, nutrition, continence, cognition, caregiver support, and home hazards.
- Balance training benefit against supervision, transfer risk, environment, fatigue, and recovery. A remote unsupervised challenge is inappropriate when a fall cannot be safely managed.

## Pain, fatigue, and long-term conditions

- Validate the person's experience without implying that pain is imaginary or that one tissue finding explains all disability.
- Separate symptom response from tissue damage, and combine education with active, individualized management where appropriate.
- Avoid rigid “perfect posture,” fear-based imaging language, guaranteed timelines, blame, and one-size-fits-all pacing.

## Amputation, prosthetics, wheelchairs, orthoses, assistive and rehabilitation technology

- Check skin integrity, pressure, fit, alignment, sensation, circulation, volume change, falls, transfer ability, device condition, environment, and manufacturer/qualified-provider requirements.
- Do not fabricate device parameters, wheelchair configuration, prosthetic alignment, electrical settings, or authorization. Changes may require the relevant prosthetist, orthotist, engineer, physician, or supplier.

## Wounds, burns, lymphatic and vascular conditions

- Consider perfusion, infection, tissue viability, sensation, oedema cause, cardiopulmonary/renal status, malignancy, thrombosis, skin fragility, and current wound/vascular orders.
- Compression, manual techniques, heat/cold, and loading are not generic interventions; verify diagnosis, contraindications, pressure/device, competence, monitoring, and stop criteria.

## Occupational, community, public-health, and prevention work

- Assess the real task, exposure, schedule, psychosocial and organizational factors, accessibility, resources, health literacy, culture, and equity—not only body position.
- Do not present ergonomic variation as proof of causation or promise that one posture/product prevents pain.
- Community recommendations must state the population, setting, implementer, resources, harms, reach, and evaluation plan.

## Remote and cross-border care

- Re-check identity, current physical location, emergency route, consent, privacy, platform, local licensure/scope, safe space, helper, equipment, camera limitations, and connection-failure plan at each relevant encounter.
- State what cannot be assessed remotely. Convert to in-person or emergency care when examination uncertainty or environment creates unacceptable risk.
